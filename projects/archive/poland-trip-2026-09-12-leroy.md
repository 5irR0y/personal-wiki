## Leroy — travel gear and services to buy / set up

Four things beyond clothes and toiletries. Two of them (the card and the phone data) have lead time, so they are dated on the timeline above.

### European outlet adapter

☑ Buy a Europe Type C/E plug adapter — Poland uses Type C/E, 230V. Bought one on 9/7; a second is still optional if cheap.

Note: a plug **adapter** is almost certainly all that is needed. Phone and laptop chargers are dual-voltage (check the brick — if it says 100–240V, it is fine). A heavy voltage **converter** is only needed for something like a US-only hair dryer or shaver. Buy two adapters if they are cheap — one for the phone, one for the laptop.

### Phone data in Europe — settled 9/10: Nomad eSIM

**Nomad eSIM**, bought and set up 9/10. The travel-eSIM route won, as expected on 9/7 — the US number stays live for calls and texts, and data runs over the eSIM.

☑ Figure out how the phone gets internet in Poland. Done 9/10 — Nomad eSIM. The options that were on the table:

- Carrier international day pass — easiest, no setup, but a daily fee for 5 days
- eSIM from a travel provider — usually cheaper, installs on the phone before leaving, keeps the US number for calls/texts
- Physical SIM bought in Poland — cheapest but means swapping the US SIM out

☑ Check whether the phone supports eSIM and is carrier-unlocked before buying anything. Done 9/10.

☑ Buy / install whichever option wins. Done 9/10 — Nomad eSIM purchased and installed.

### Credit card with no foreign transaction fees

☑ Card for personal spending — souvenirs and misc. **Settled 9/8 — no new card.** Use the Capital One Mastercard or the Discover card; work expenses go on the company card, which also has no foreign transaction fee.

☑ Check the foreign transaction fee on the Capital One Mastercard and the Discover card. Done 9/10 — **neither charges one**, so fees are not the deciding factor. Acceptance is: Mastercard is taken in more places in Poland than Discover, so lead with the Capital One and keep Discover as the backup. Both are coming along.

☑ Tell the card issuers about the travel dates so nothing gets frozen. Done 9/10.

Note: Poland uses the złoty (PLN), not the euro. Cards work nearly everywhere, but carry a little cash for small stuff. If a card reader offers to charge in dollars instead of złoty, decline — the exchange rate is worse.
